Really, if you think about it, what even is the truth? Is it merely some empirically validated and statistically guaranteed observation about our physical world? Or is it a chain of logically proven statements to arrive at a tautology? What about things that you cannot properly define. Or things that are beyond the realm of what can be conveyed. What is truth?

And so, I spent my days slaving away at an office building trying to figure out what the truth was. I tried many a things.

Eventually, I realized there was far too much news and somehow never enough information.

Every newspaper had its own account of Nepal. Sometimes they disagreed. Sometimes one of them covered a story that the others seemed to pretend had never happened. I had no grand solution for deciding who was right, but I thought it would at least help if people could see more than one version of the country.

That was the idea behind EkNepal, a platform I helped build while working with Alpinist Studios.

EkNepal brought reporting from Nepal's major newspapers into one place. I worked on the frontend: the part that turned all of that scattered information into something people could actually navigate, read, and compare. You can still look it up at [eknepal.com](https://eknepal.com/).

It was my first programming gig. I was paid one hundred dollars.

Honestly, I was just amazed that somebody had paid me to make something. Until then, programming was mostly me messing around with a computer. Now there was a real website, real people could visit it, and some little piece of it had come from a question that would not leave me alone.

The questions I work on now are different. They involve algorithms, mathematical proofs, experiments, and occasionally several days spent discovering that an idea does not work. But the habit is the same. I find something confusing, pull it apart, build whatever I can, and see what survives.

EkNepal did not tell anyone what the truth was. I doubt any website could.

But really, if truth rarely arrives in one piece, why do we keep accepting it from a single source?
